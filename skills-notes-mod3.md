Module 3 Skills Notes
Posting 1: Data Scientist I or II
Industry: Semiconductor technology
Seniority: Intermediate to experienced

Required skills:
-Python programming
-Machine learning and statistical modeling
-Neural networks and patterns recognition
-Time series analysis and anomaly detection
-Working with large datasets
-AI and large language model systems
-Problem solving and analytic thinking
-Communication and teamwork
-Software development and testing
-A related master's degree and about five years of experience

Preferred or additional skills:
-Distributed systems
-Machine learning infrastructure
-MLOps
-Signal processing

Posting 2: Human Resources Data Analyst II - HRIS
Industry: Public education and human resources
Seniority: Mid level analyst

Required skills:
-Working with and organizing data
-Databases and SQL
-Spreadsheets and Microsoft Office
-Creating reports
-Data entry, cleaning, and checking accuracy
-HR and payroll systems
-Communication and customer service
-Teamwork and problem solving
-Confidentiality and recordkeeping
-A related bachelor's degree and relevant experience

Preferred skills:
-Two years of HR management system experience
-Experience in K-12 education or the public sector
-Experience working as an HR data analyst

Skills found in both postings:
-Working with data
-SQL and databases
-Problem solving
-Communication
-Teamwork
-Using technology to solve problems

What stood out to me:
I noticed that both jobs involve working with data, but the actual work is pretty different. The data scientist job focuses more on AI, programming, and machine learning while the HR analyst job focuses more on reports, databases, and employee information. I was also surprised that both jobs require good communication skills and teamwork.
